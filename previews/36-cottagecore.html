The First Strawberry

June 14, 2026 — afternoon light

There is nothing quite like the first strawberry of the season. Warm from the sun, still dusted with soil, it tastes of patience and promise. I picked it at dawn and ate it sitting on the garden wall, watching the bees wake among the lavender.

The roses are climbing well this year. I've tied new canes to the trellis and whispered a little encouragement to each one.

“The earth laughs in flowers. To sit in the garden is to be present at the most patient conversation.”

A Little About Me

I live in a stone cottage at the edge of a hazel wood. I keep bees, write letters, and dry flowers between the pages of old books. This journal is where I gather what is soft and true.

— Elara

A Walk to the Beech Grove

June 8, 2026 — after the rain

The air after rain carries a sweetness you can taste. I walked the lane to the beech grove, where the canopy filters the light into a green cathedral. A fox crossed the path ahead and paused to look back at me, as if to say, you are welcome here.

I collected a few fallen beech leaves for pressing and a feather from a jay. Small treasures. The day's true harvest was quietness.
